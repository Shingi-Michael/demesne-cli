# Changelog

Current release status is **0.1.0 plus unreleased development on main**. The entries below summarize delivered code as of 2026-10-05; they do not claim a new published release. See the [documentation index](docs/README.md) for current setup and commands.

## [Unreleased]

- The browser's tabs close. Each tab chip has its own ×; the new tab's × goes back to the page it was opened over. The last page closes with × in the bar, as before. Read more: [Tabs, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-views).
- The browser has a proper bar (pass 256 A). Back, forward, reload, the address, **New tab** (+), **Open in your browser** (↗) and × are always there, and using any of them makes the page yours, the same as scrolling it. What the model is doing moved into the bar as a few words at the end of the address, amber when it asks you to sign in or the page is yours; the line under the page ("The model is done with this page…") is gone. A loading page shows a thin silver line along the bar. A new tab, and the browser before the model opens anything, shows just the Demesne logo; type an address and it opens in a tab of its own. Read more: [Toolbars, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars), [Designing empty states, NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/).
- The browser hands over by itself. **Take over** and **Hand back** are gone: scroll, click or type on the page and it is yours, and the model waits; click in the chat or start typing there and it goes back to the model, which reads the page again. When the model asks you to sign in, going back to the chat is how you say you're done. Read more: [implicit interaction](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/direct-manipulation/).
- The browser's other tabs no longer show over the chat on a Mac. A tab not on show was put away inside a one-point holder, which the Mac does not clip, so the whole page showed in the window's top-left corner. It is now put away with all but one point of it outside the window, under a one-point cover. Read more: [`View.setBounds`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/view#viewsetboundsbounds).
- The browser beside the chat is a real browser now. The split used to show a stream of pictures of a page that ran in a hidden copy of the app, which is why it could feel slow, choppy and soft. While the app is open the model's browser now runs inside the app's own window, and the split shows the page itself: scrolling, typing and text are as fast and sharp as in any browser, and while the page is yours your mouse and keys go straight to it. While the model has the page it takes no input from you. The hidden browser stays for when the app is not open. See [docs/browser.md](docs/browser.md). Read more: [`WebContentsView`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/web-contents-view), [Chromium's multi-process architecture](https://www.chromium.org/developers/design-documents/multi-process-architecture/).
- Demesne remembers what you teach it (passes 249 A, 254, 255). When you correct the model or show a lasting preference, it keeps one short line, quietly, in one of two halves: **About you** (`~/.demesne/memory/MEMORY.md`, every project) or **this project** (`.demesne/memory/MEMORY.md`). When the turn ends, a silver pill above the composer says "Demesne will remember 3 things", with a silver light around its edge for as long as it is up; click it to see what, drop one line, or Undo all. Each chat reads both halves once when it starts, so its prompt cache keeps holding. Rules you say in words still go to `DEMESNE.md`. See [docs/memory.md](docs/memory.md). Read more: [how Claude remembers your project](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory), [components of agent memory](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory).
- The browser beside the chat is crisp on a Retina screen: its hidden page now draws at the screen's own sharpness (two device pixels per page pixel on a Mac), where it drew one and was stretched. Read more: [`devicePixelRatio`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/devicePixelRatio).
- The browser beside the chat is readable and smooth to use. A page used to be laid out 1280 wide and shrunk to fit the split, which made text tiny; it is now laid out at the split's own width and shown at its own size (the **Fit / 100%** switch is gone). While the page is yours it sends 30 pictures a second instead of 10, and a scroll shows in about 64 ms instead of 112. `xvfb-run -a bun scripts/check-browser-smooth.ts` measures both. Read more: [responsive design and the viewport](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Core/CSS_layout/Responsive_Design).
- Pages in the browser follow Demesne's theme: a site with a dark version shows it when Demesne is dark, and its light one when Demesne is light, whatever the computer is set to. Sites with one look keep it. Read more: [`prefers-color-scheme`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-color-scheme).
- The browser beside the chat no longer flickers when its page scrolls. Each new picture replaced the last as an image, and the image went blank while the next one loaded: about a quarter of the time while scrolling. Pictures are now drawn on a canvas only once they are ready, over the last one. `xvfb-run -a bun scripts/check-browser-scroll.ts` photographs the split while a page scrolls and fails on a blank. Read more: [`HTMLImageElement.decode()`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLImageElement/decode), [drawing images on a canvas](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Using_images).
- The model's browser is fast again. Hidden pages drew 60 frames a second even with nobody watching, and each picture for the split was captured and encoded on the browser's main thread. Now a hidden page draws 10 frames a second (12 while you watch), the split's pictures stream from the page itself (CDP screencast, up to ten a second), and public sites' requests are no longer followed. On an animated page the browser's CPU went from 89% to 16% of a core unwatched, 128% to 60% watched. Read more: [offscreen rendering](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/tutorial/offscreen-rendering), [`Page.startScreencast`](https://chromedevtools.github.io/devtools-protocol/tot/Page/#method-startScreencast).
- The Mac app opens in full screen. It used to open as a small window and then grow, and the first minute after that growth could be slow; now it is full screen from the first frame it shows, on the desktop's Space so the glass still shows the wallpaper. The green light gives you the window at its own size. Read more: [`setSimpleFullScreen`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/browser-window#winsetsimplefullscreenflag-macos).
- The model's browser does more. A ring shows where it clicked; **Fit / 100%** shows the page at its own size to read it; **Take over** gives you the page any time, with an address bar, back, forward and reload, and the model waits and then hears you used it; **Pages** steps back through what it saw; a "Sign in with…" popup or a new-tab link opens as another tab (chips above the page), and the model can list, switch and close tabs; `upload` gives a page project files and downloads land in `.demesne/downloads/`; session sign-ins last a restart; and on this computer's pages the model can read what the server answered and how a control is styled. See [docs/browser.md](docs/browser.md). Read more: [CDP `Network.getResponseBody`](https://chromedevtools.github.io/devtools-protocol/tot/Network/#method-getResponseBody), [`setWindowOpenHandler`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/window-open), [Electron `DownloadItem`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/download-item).
- The model's browser is beside the chat (pass 248 D). The first time a turn browses, the window splits: the chat on the left, the model's own browser on the right, live, with the address and what the model is doing. When a site needs a person, the model hands you the page (`hand_over`): sign in on it, press Hand back, and the model reads the page again without ever seeing what you typed. The page stays after the answer until you close it with ×, a browser step opens it again, and you can drag the line between them. Each project keeps its own browser sign-ins on disk, so you sign in once. See [docs/browser.md](docs/browser.md). Read more: [visibility of system status](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visibility-system-status/), [Electron offscreen rendering](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/tutorial/offscreen-rendering), [`persist:` partitions](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/session#sessionfrompartitionpartition-options).
- The chat no longer flickers while a model works. The blue light that runs around the panel during a turn was a gradient whose angle moved, which made the app paint the whole chat panel again about 60 times a second for as long as the turn ran; on the Mac's see-through window that showed as the whole chat screen flickering. The light is now a sheet that turns behind the rim, so only its position changes and nothing is painted again: it looks the same. `xvfb-run -a bun scripts/check-rim-steady.ts` counts the panel's paints during a turn in the real app. Read more: [how browsers render a frame](https://web.dev/articles/rendering-performance), [animations that stay on the compositor](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide).
- The Mac app opens again on Apple silicon. Its Bun backends shipped with a signature that no longer matched them, so macOS killed the backend the moment the app started it ("Desktop backend exited (SIGKILL)"), after a fresh install and after every update. `bun run build:desktop` now signs them ad hoc and checks every signature, the release refuses to package a Mac app whose signatures don't check, and `install.sh` re-signs an older download that needs it.
- The context card says how the chat's cache is doing (pass 243 A). Under how full the chat is, a Cache half says how much of this chat the provider read from its cache, how long it stays warm on this model (or that it has gone cold, and that the next message reads it once again), a grey tick per request with the ones that were read again faint, and the last miss in one sentence. Nothing new shows until you open the context word. See `docs/caching.md`.
- Every provider keeps a chat's cache. On OpenRouter each chat carries its own key, so it stays on the provider that holds its cache, and Claude, Qwen and DeepSeek on Alibaba, which cache only when asked, are asked; OpenAI by key gets the chat's key too. A model that refuses the ask is sent the same request without it. Old tool results are no longer shortened below the window on providers by key, which made them read the rest of the chat again. See `docs/caching.md`.
- A turn that runs out of its allowance keeps its cache. The last request of such a turn, the one that asks the model where it stands, used to go out with no tools and an extra sentence in the instructions, so the provider read the whole turn again, and the follow-up after it too. It now starts exactly like the request before it and is told in a note at the end that tools are not run; a tool it asks for anyway is not run. A sub-agent's last round keeps its tools the same way. See `docs/caching.md`.
- A cache clock per provider. Demesne used to treat every provider's cache as gone after a fixed 15 minutes (for cutting old turns) or 10 (for compacting while you are away). It now knows how long each provider keeps its cache: 30 minutes on GPT-5.6 and later, 5 on Claude, Gemini and earlier GPT models, and on a model of your own machine time alone never makes it cold. It asks for the 12-hour lifetime on GPT-5.6 and later (and stops asking on a route that refuses it), and corrects all of it with what its own cache meter measured over the last 14 days. See [docs/caching.md](docs/caching.md).
- Old turns are shortened only when it costs nothing. The source of a Foundry page used to leave the chat as soon as its turn was over, which rewrote a turn the provider's cache still held, so the request after every page was read again from there. It now leaves with the old file reads, at a cut that moves only once the chat has sat idle long enough for the cache to be gone. Pictures a seeing model gets no longer drop one each time a new one arrives: two to four ride along and older ones leave three at a time. On the prefix check one request still breaks the prefix, the first after 20 minutes away. See [docs/caching.md](docs/caching.md).
- Tools that wait are called without changing the tool list. Tools from MCP servers, and Demesne's own beyond the everyday ones on a small-window model, used to join a chat's tool list when `tool_search` loaded them, which made the provider (or your own machine, for a local model) read the whole chat again. Now `tool_search` answers with how a tool is called and a fixed `use_tool` calls it, so the list a chat starts with is the list it keeps. The step in the chat, its approval and its checks are the real tool's, as before. See [docs/caching.md](docs/caching.md).
- Tools no longer join a chat's tool list later. On a model with a large window all of Demesne's own tools go out whole from the first request (about 7,500 more tokens, read from the provider's cache after that), so using Foundry, git history, threads or any other tool for the first time no longer makes the provider read the whole chat again, and no round is spent on `tool_search`. On a small window they still wait behind `tool_search`, as do tools from MCP servers. `recall_turn` and `read_source` are in every chat from the start. On the scripted chat of the prefix check a cache can now reuse 94% of what is sent (56% two steps ago). See [docs/caching.md](docs/caching.md).
- One fixed start per chat: a chat's instructions and tool list no longer change while you use it, so the provider's prompt cache keeps the whole conversation when you switch plan mode, auto-approve or Accept edits, when Drive or the other agents change, when a turn runs on its own, and when a long chat starts keeping its hidden notes. Each of those is now told to the model as a short note sent with your message. Plan mode keeps every tool in the list and refuses edits and commands where they run, as it already did. On the scripted chat of the prefix check a cache can now reuse 86% of what is sent, up from 56%. `bun scripts/cache-report.ts` now finds your history on its own (`demesne.sqlite`). See [docs/caching.md](docs/caching.md).
- Prompt caching is measured: every model request records how much of the chat's previous request it repeats from its first byte (all a provider's cache can reuse), and what changed when it does not. `bun scripts/check-cache-prefix.ts` fails on a new break and runs with the tests; `bun scripts/cache-report.ts` reads the hit rate, how long each provider keeps its cache, and why requests missed from your own history. See [docs/caching.md](docs/caching.md).
- The checklist ribbon is cleaner (pass 226 A): one soft glass ribbon with the name of the step it is on, which step that is ("2 of 5"), and a fine line under them that fills in green a step at a time. Silver is now, green is done. Click the ribbon and the list opens inside it. The name rolls once when a step finishes, and when the turn ends the same ribbon shrinks into a small pill ("5 steps") that still opens the list.
- Threads are checked as they land (pass 211 A): a thread that changed files outside its claim or failed a check goes back once with what to fix, then the orchestrator reads its work against the brief and can send it back once more. Each ready thread has its own Merge, a thread that no longer applies settles its own conflicts, up to six threads run at once, a thread can wait for another (`after`) and start on its branch, and threads hang under their orchestrator in the sidebar. See [Threads](docs/threads.md).
- Every new chat starts on **Auto**: edits and commands run without asking. The mode word under the composer still switches a chat to Ask, Accept edits or Plan.
- **Just a chat** can make changes: a chat with no project works in your home folder, reading and editing files and running commands there (paths like `Desktop/notes.md`). Demesne's own data folder and config stay out of its file tools, and key folders (`.ssh`, `.aws`, `.gnupg`) stay hidden as before. Threads and remembered rules still need a project.

- A working thread can be stopped: its composer's send turns into Stop while the thread works, as in any chat, and turns back to send once you type (words steer it instead).
- Drive lives at the foot of the sidebar, above you and Settings: its state, what it's on, Tell Drive and its ideas unfold there, and nothing sits above the composer any more. Branches to review stay on the sidebar's Review line.

### Changed

- Foundry pages look designed again: the brief no longer asks for "little CSS of your own" on top of the kit (pages since the kit used about a third as much styling, no fonts and no motion). It now treats the kit as the floor and asks for a direction, one focal point, a real type scale, one strong move per page, depth where it earns it, and two or three named directions side by side when you ask for options.
- Old reads leave the chat once they're old: when earlier turns' long read-style results (read_file/read_files, search, listings, git diff/history/status, command output over 1,500 characters) pass about 60k tokens, every turn but the last two is cut, in one step, to a short note of what was read (the start of the text, the paths, a command's last lines); the model reads again if it needs them. The last two turns stay whole, so a follow-up about what was just read still answers from the cached copy (trimming those too cost more rounds and uncached tokens in a live test). Cuts are rare and worked out from the history alone, so the cached prefix holds between them.
- Opening a chat whose model the daemon can't switch to no longer takes the whole window offline: the chat opens on the model already running. check-desktop follows today's changes (thinking starts on Medium, the groove rounds to the nearest level, an edit's diff opens as its own view).
- Fewer rounds on the Codex plan: the Codex route asked for one tool call per round (`parallel_tool_calls: false`), so 535 of 547 tool rounds in two weeks made a single call while the prompt asked the model to batch its reads. It now allows several. A round runs up to 8 (read-only ones together); calls past 8 come back "not run" so the model asks again, instead of the turn failing. Tool calls whose arguments arrive whole at the end, with no streamed pieces (the Codex route does this with parallel calls on), are now read instead of failing the turn with "conflicting tool call data".
- The working cube is back in place: the working line no longer clips its left side while it turns, and the cube now sits under the answer's d. mark with the silver words lined up with the answer text.
- Permissions: the rules it no longer asks about sit in groups by what they run (grep 30, git 15, edits 2), one short line each, instead of a wall of long commands that wrapped over each other. A click on a group unfolds its rules as one-line rows (the full text on hover), each with × to ask again. Past three one-off rules, they share one "other" chip, and file rules say what they allow (edit, delete) so two rules on one file read apart.
- Talk instead of typing (Mac): a microphone beside + in the composer starts the Mac's own Dictation in the composer. Your words appear as you say them (grey until they settle), stay yours to edit, and nothing sends until you press Enter. Holding or double-pressing fn (the Mac's Dictation shortcut) works the same.
- Ask again: the chat's last answer has **Ask again** (or **Ask again on** the model now in the composer, when you switched). The same message is answered once more without the first answer in view, and the answers share one spot with ‹ 1 of 2 › to compare; the model reads only the one you leave showing, and the chat goes on from it. Answers that changed files, messages with images, and messages the chat has gone on from aren't offered (Rewind handles those). New daemon routes `POST /v1/sessions/:id/again` and `/versions`, and the session state's `versions`.
- Build this: an HTML Foundry page's line has **Build this**, which asks the chat to turn the page into the project's real code, reading the page with `foundry_read` and checking its work against it. A page from Just a chat first asks which project it's for (the Move to a project choices open) and starts building once the chat is there.
- Pick an option on a Foundry page: when a page shows options (marked `data-option="B · Joined"`, which the model now does), hovering one shows a small Pick button; a click sends "I pick B · Joined" to the chat (after the running turn, if one is working), so the next version starts from it.
- Big pastes fold up: paste 30 lines or more (or a very long line) into the composer and it becomes one chip, "Pasted text · 840 lines", instead of a wall of text; × takes it back. It's sent whole, and the sent message shows the same chip, which unfolds on a click.
- Since you looked: open a chat that worked while you were elsewhere and a line before the first turn you didn't see says what happened (turns finished, files changed, how the checks went, what waits for you), worked out from the turns with no model call. The chat opens at that line so you read on from where you left.
- The Dock shows Demesne working: while any chat, queued chat or Drive mission works, the icon's progress bar runs; it clears once nothing works. The badge still counts the chats waiting on you.
- Answer from the notification: when a chat waits on you while Demesne is in the background, its banner says the question itself and carries its suggestions as buttons and a reply field, or Allow once and Deny for an approval. Your pick or reply goes straight to that chat, open or not.
- When a chat changes Demesne's own source, its last changing turn says whether the app you're in runs that change yet: "Changed, not running yet" until the app's build is newer than the turn, then "Running now". On a Mac, **Rebuild and open** builds the app from the chat's folder, waits until nothing is working, swaps it in (keeping the old one as Demesne.app.prev, put back if the copy fails) and reopens. A folder that doesn't have the commit the app was built from says so instead of offering to rebuild.
- Questions: a set of questions has Back, which takes your last answer back to change it (a typed answer returns to the field). While the agent works past an open question, the end of its steps says "Waits for your answer" with the question, and a click goes to the card.
- A chat's tab gets a real name after its first answer: the chat's own model (thinking off) names it in 2 to 4 words from what was asked and answered ("Tab names", not the first prompt cut at sixty characters). Only chats a person is in, only once, and never over a name you gave it meanwhile; a slow or odd reply leaves the first-prompt name.
- The app runs on Electron 44.10.0 (from 44.7.2: Chromium and Node security and bug fixes), with electron-builder 26.18 and sharp 0.35.5.
- While the model works, the silver line under the turn says what it's doing and moves on as the work does: the heading of its latest thought, the step running now ("Reading live.ts"), fast steps counted ("Reading 5 files"), and what it has read so far. It is one line kept out of the turn's repaints, each phrase stays at least 2.4 seconds, phrases glide, and the shimmer slides over a bright copy of the words instead of repainting them, so it doesn't stutter. The thinking row and running steps no longer shine on their own.
- You don't have to say "Foundry": the model decides by the shape of the best answer (something to look at, click or compare gets a page, made in that turn), `foundry_save` reads as "show the person something", plan mode can make pages, and Drive, routines and mail make one only when asked. `scripts/eval-foundry-inference.ts` scores it against your own model.
- The Foundry pane is one line: the title (it opens the Foundry page), the version arrows with the version's note on hover, and icons for code, phone width, export and close. The line under the page is gone, and with it Revise (the composer does that), the separate Copy (it's in code view) and Open in Foundry (the title). An artifact keeps one chip in the chat; a later change shows "Edited Idea garden · v2" instead of another chip. A plain chat's closing line no longer says "Create in Foundry here".
- The app starts the daemon it bundles: it looked for it one folder too high, so starting a daemon fell back to whatever `demesned` was on PATH (possibly an older install), and replacing a daemon from another build never happened.
- The app keeps its daemon current: a daemon left running from an older build of the app is restarted from the bundled one once nothing is working, instead of serving old code until someone restarts it by hand. Builds now carry an id (commit and build time) that `/healthz` reports as `build`.
- The motion line's buttons no longer get rebuilt on every tick of a playing motion, so a click on Pause or a motion name that lands mid-motion isn't lost.
- A Foundry page that moves by itself (the kit's anime.js) no longer gets the first-show assembly on top of its own motion: the two animated the same pieces, anime.js cancelled the page's, and the page was left faded and short of its marks with the motion line stuck near the start.
- The agent offers Foundry while it works: when the question is how something should look or flow, it says it will mock it in Foundry and makes it, instead of describing it in words, and offers a mockup when one would help you decide.
- A Foundry page that names its motions (`demesne.motion("Arrive", timeline)`, in the kit's anime.js) gets one thin line under the page in the pane: play or pause, a scrubber, the motions by name, half speed, Replay. The frame and the pane talk by postMessage; a page without named motions shows no line.
- Foundry pages start from a kit served from inside the app (`foundry://kit/base.css`: Inter, one ink and its tints, hairlines, radii, light and dark, buttons, inputs, tables, cards; `foundry://kit/anime.js` for motion), and the design brief tells the model to lean on it: content, layout and its own accent, little CSS of its own. Pages read as one hand and cost fewer tokens to write; Export inlines the kit so the file stands alone.
- Foundry costs a chat less: the tools' text is a third shorter and no longer repeated in the system prompt, and a page's source leaves the history once the turn that made or read it is over (a one-line stub names the artifact; `foundry_read` brings the text back). A 20 KB page used to ride along with every request for the rest of its chat.
- Home's cherry blossom is a still anime-style drawing: two boughs with cel-shaded blossoms, a moon and stars at night, a blue sky with clouds by day. The swaying photographed boughs and falling petals are gone, and with them the start screen's constant redrawing.
- The agent's questions live in the chat and no longer stop it: a clarification is a card at the step that asked it, and the agent keeps doing whatever doesn't depend on the answer. Answers are picked in the card (with what each choice means, several at once where the question allows, or your own words in its field); the composer stays a composer. The answer reaches a running turn after its current step, or starts the next one, and the question folds into one "✓ Asked · question · answer" line. The card springs in word by word (anime.js), amber only on its ring, top edge and suggested answer. Interviews such as `/themefy` still wait for each answer.

### Fixed

- The assistant's Windows tasks no longer die when a Windows Terminal window is closed. With Windows Terminal as the default terminal, a task started as `powershell.exe -WindowStyle Hidden` was handed to Terminal as a tab, and closing that window sent Ctrl+C to the daemon and the model server at once (three times in a week). `install.ps1` now starts each task inside a headless console host (`conhost.exe --headless`), which has no window and never joins Terminal. Because stopping a task then ends only that console host, `keep-running.ps1` watches it and exits with it, so Stop-ScheduledTask still stops the daemon or the model server.
- On the Mac, Demesne comes to the front when it opens, once. Opened from a terminal or script it could stay behind the app that launched it, with its window present but hidden.
- A chat whose project folder was removed (a deleted worktree) no longer makes the daemon throw ENOENT when the window lists its files; the daemon answers "The project folder is gone".
- The daemon log no longer grows without end: past 5 MB it moves to `daemon.log.1` (replacing the one before) when a daemon starts. One Mac's had reached 28 MB.
- The green button goes straight to full screen on the Mac: the window no longer grows, snaps back and grows again.
- The buttons drawn in Mac full screen work with the sidebar open too: its drag region was still taking their clicks.
- The assistant's Windows tasks and the window no longer fight over the daemon. `install.ps1` writes `daemon.managed` in the data directory, naming the task as the daemon's owner; while it exists the window waits for that daemon instead of starting one of its own, whatever `[daemon] auto_start` says; **Start daemon** says who runs it, and Settings › **Restart the daemon** stops it and waits for the task to bring it back. - The assistant's chat runs only on its own model: the `[provider]` model, or the one `[assistant] model` names. Before, the window's model picker switched the daemon's model for every chat at once, so picking Codex in any chat sent the assistant's next routine, and the files it read, to Codex until the daemon restarted; a dropped provider could do the same mid-turn. Now the assistant's turns are pinned, and one that loses its provider fails instead of continuing elsewhere.
- The assistant's chat moves into its folder when the folder is trusted a moment after the chat was first opened, instead of staying without `DEMESNE.md`, notes or a to-do list for good.
- The assistant's chat no longer shows Drive: no "Drive is choosing the first step…" in an empty chat, no Drive word in its title bar, no Drive line or branch notes, and the model there is told nothing of Drive.
