# The model's own browser

Demesne has a browser that belongs to the model. It is a hidden window the model uses to check that the web app it is working on really works, the way a person would, by clicking through it, and to use a site on the internet when reading it is not enough. You don't drive it, but you can watch it beside the chat, and when a site needs a person (a sign-in) the model hands the page to you.

It is the `browser` tool. It is different from `look_at_page`, which takes one fresh look and forgets:

- **It stays open.** Each chat has one hidden tab that keeps its page, cookies and place between the model's calls. The model can sign in, go several pages deep, and come back. Another chat (or a thread) has its own tab and its own cookies. A tab left alone for 20 minutes is closed, and deleting a chat closes its tab.
- **It reads structure, not pixels.** A page comes back as an outline: its headings, and every control on a numbered line.

  ```
  # Sign in
  [1] textbox "Name"
  [2] button "Sign in"
  [3] link "The list"  → /list
  ```

  The model says `click` with `ref: 2`. A number holds for as long as that control is on the page, so the outline is sent again only when the address changes (or the model asks with `read`). A control's visible name works too.
- **It says what happened.** After each action the model is told what a person would have seen change ("new text “Signed in as Ada”", "went to /list", "nothing visible changed"), plus any new console errors and failed requests (`GET /api/save → 500`). A page's `alert`, `confirm` or `prompt` is answered and reported, since nobody is there to press OK.
- **Pictures only when asked.** `picture` returns a screenshot, at most six a turn. The outline and `text` (the page's words, 6,000 characters at a time) are far cheaper.
- **It can hand the page to you.** `hand_over` (with a `reason`, like "Sign in to the Slow Pour admin") gives you the open page for something only you should do: a sign-in, a code sent to your phone, a captcha. The model waits until you go back to the chat, then reads the page again. It never sees what you typed, and it is told never to type your password or ask for it in the chat.

Actions: `open`, `read`, `text`, `click`, `type` (with `clear` and `enter`), `key`, `hover`, `scroll`, `back`, `forward`, `reload`, `wait` (for text to appear, or a time), `picture`, `hand_over`, `close`.

## Beside the chat (pass 248 D)

The first time a turn uses the browser, the window splits: the chat on the left, the model's browser on the right, live. It shows the page the model is on under a browser bar (pass 256 A).

- **The bar.** Back, forward, reload, the address, **New tab** (+), **Open in your browser** (↗, the address goes to your default browser) and × are always there. Using any of them makes the page yours, the same as scrolling it. What the model is doing shows as a few grey words with a blue dot at the end of the address ("Clicking “Pay”"); a sign-in ask, or a page you hold, shows in amber ("Yours · the model waits"). Once the model is done the bar is just a browser bar: no words, and no line under the page. While a page loads, a thin silver line runs along the bottom of the bar. Read more: [Toolbars, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars), [Visibility of system status, NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visibility-system-status/).
- **Home is just the logo.** A new tab, and the browser before the model has opened a page, show only the Demesne logo, quiet in the middle. Type an address in the bar and it opens in a tab of its own; Back or Escape returns to the page the tab was opened over. Read more: [Designing empty states, NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/).

- **Watching costs the model nothing.** The model still reads the page as an outline. The pictures are for you: while the split shows, the page streams JPEGs of itself (Chromium draws them small and encodes them, and sends the next only once the last is taken), at most ten a second, and stops a few seconds after the split closes. The page is drawn as wide and as tall as the split.
- **Pages follow Demesne's light or dark.** A site that has a dark version (`prefers-color-scheme`) shows it when Demesne's theme is dark, and its light one when Demesne is light, whatever the computer is set to. A site with only one look keeps it: nothing is recoloured, so the model checks pages as people see them. Read more: [`prefers-color-scheme`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-color-scheme), [Electron `nativeTheme`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/native-theme).
- **A hidden page draws sparingly.** An offscreen page copies every frame it draws, so a tab nobody watches draws 10 frames a second and a watched one 12, not 60. On this computer's pages the browser follows requests (for `requests` and `response`); on public sites it doesn't. Measured on an animated page: 89% of a core to 16% unwatched, 128% to 60% watched.
- **A sign-in is yours.** When the model hands the page over, the end of the address turns amber and says what to do. Click into the page and type: your clicks, scrolling, keys and pastes go to the page, and only while it is yours. Click in the chat when you are done. If you don't within 15 minutes, or the turn is stopped, the model is told and carries on without it.
- **It stays until you close it.** After the answer the last page stays, so you can look at it. × gives the chat the whole window back; the split then stays closed for that turn, and opens again the next time a turn browses, when the model hands you a page, or when you click one of the browser's steps. Drag the line between chat and browser to change its width; the width is kept.
- **Google signs in in your own browser.** Google refuses to sign anyone in inside a browser built into an app ("This browser or app may not be secure"), and passkeys saved in your Mac's Keychain can't reach it either. So when a page goes to Google's sign-in (a "Sign in with Google" button, a popup, or the model opening it), the page you were on opens in your own browser instead, where its Google button works and its own sign-in steps stay together. The bar says so in amber. Demesne then watches your browsers for that site, and once you've signed in there it brings the site's sign-in into the project's browser (as Settings › Browser does) and reloads the page: "Signed in to figma.com from Chrome". It watches for ten minutes. Your Google account itself never comes in, and a chat outside a project has nowhere to keep it. Read more: [Google on embedded browsers](https://developers.googleblog.com/2016/08/modernizing-oauth-interactions-in-native-apps.html), [OAuth 2.0 for native apps, RFC 8252](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8252).
- **Sign-ins are kept per project.** Each project has its own browser profile on disk (`browser-profile/` beside Demesne's database), shared by its chats, so you sign in to a site once. A sign-in stays after a restart, including one that lasts only for the session (it is saved when Demesne quits). A chat outside a project has its own cookies, kept in memory only.
- **Bring sign-ins in from your other browsers** (pass 261 A). Settings › Browser lists the sites you're signed in to in Chrome, Arc, Brave, Edge, Safari or Firefox, used last first, and brings the ones you tick into the open project's browser, so its chats can use them without signing in. Email and money sites show greyed and can't be ticked: the model can use whatever comes in. Listing reads only site names and dates; bringing sites in reads their cookies. Chrome, Arc, Brave and Edge lock them with a key in the Mac's Keychain, so the Mac asks once (Always Allow stops it asking again); Safari's need Full Disk Access for Demesne; Firefox's are plain. On Windows, Chromium browsers lock their cookies to themselves, so only Firefox works there. What comes in is a copy taken then; **In this project** lists it, and Remove signs the project's browser out of that site. Every profile with sign-ins is its own choice under **From**, named as you named it in that browser ("Chrome · Work", "Firefox · Personal"); a browser with one profile is just its name. A Chromium browser's profiles share its one key, so the Mac still asks once. Read more: [HTTP cookies](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Guides/Cookies), [Electron `cookies`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/cookies), [Keychain Access](https://support.apple.com/guide/keychain-access/what-is-keychain-access-kyca1083/mac).

Read more: [Visibility of system status](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visibility-system-status/) (why showing what the model is doing matters), [offscreen rendering in Electron](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/tutorial/offscreen-rendering) (how a hidden page still paints, and why its frame rate matters), [CDP `Page.startScreencast`](https://chromedevtools.github.io/devtools-protocol/tot/Page/#method-startScreencast) (how the page streams its pictures), [`webContents.sendInputEvent`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/web-contents#contentssendinputeventinputevent) (how your clicks and keys reach it), [sessions and `persist:` partitions](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/session#sessionfrompartitionpartition-options) (how a project's cookies are kept on disk), and [HTTP cookies](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Guides/Cookies) (why a session cookie ends when the browser does, and a persistent one does not).

## The page itself, in the window

While the desktop app is open, the split does not show pictures of the page. It shows the page.

The model's browser used to run only in a hidden copy of the app, which sent the split a stream of small pictures: up to 30 a second, each one compressed, sent through the daemon and drawn again. That is why it could feel slow and soft however much it was tuned. Now, when the app is open, the browser runs inside the app's own window, and each tab is a real page of that window, placed exactly on the split's page area.

- **It is as fast and sharp as any browser.** Scrolling, typing, video and text are drawn by the page itself, at your screen's own sharpness, with nothing in between.
- **Scroll it and it is yours.** There are no buttons to take the page or give it back. Your first scroll, click or key on the page makes it yours: the model's next browser step waits, and your mouse and keys go straight to the page (select and copy work as usual). A click in the chat, or the composer taking your typing, gives it back to the model, which reads the page again before acting. The model's own clicks never count as yours: they are told apart as they are sent. When the model asks you for something (a sign-in), the page is already yours, and going back to the chat is how you say you're done.
- **Where the model clicks is marked on the page** for a moment, drawn over it without changing it.
- **The model still drives every chat's page.** A chat that is not on show, or a split you closed with ×, keeps its page open, put away inside the window at the model's own 1280 width. Its sign-ins are the same ones the hidden browser keeps (`browser-profile/`).
- **The hidden browser is still there** for when the app is not open (the terminal, Drive on a server), and takes over by itself. `DEMESNE_BROWSER=hidden` keeps the old behaviour always.

Settings and other pages over the chat put the real page away until you come back.

Read more: [`WebContentsView`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/web-contents-view) (a real page as part of a window), [Chromium's multi-process architecture](https://www.chromium.org/developers/design-documents/multi-process-architecture/) (why each page is its own process, and why streaming pictures between them costs so much), [`webContents` `input-event`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/web-contents#event-input-event) (how the page knows you scrolled or clicked it), and [Unix domain sockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix_domain_socket) (how the daemon talks to the open app).

## More of a real browser

- **Where it clicked.** A soft blue ring shows on the page where the model last clicked, hovered or typed, and fades in about a second and a half.
- **The page fits the split.** While you watch, a desktop-width page is laid out at the width the split shows it, so it is shown at its own size: nothing is shrunk, and a responsive site arranges itself for the room it has. Drag the line between chat and browser and the page follows. When nobody watches, the page is 1280 wide again for the model's own work; a page the model opened at phone width keeps it.
- **As sharp as your screen.** A hidden tab draws at the sharpness of the screen the split is on (two device pixels per page pixel on a Retina screen), so text in the split is crisp and sites serve their high-resolution images. It is fixed when a tab is made: a Mac is taken as Retina until a split has said otherwise. Read more: [`devicePixelRatio`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/devicePixelRatio), [offscreen rendering's `deviceScaleFactor`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/tutorial/offscreen-rendering).
- **Smooth while it is yours.** A page you hold draws and sends 30 pictures a second (12 while you only watch), and the next picture is asked for at once. Measured in the real app: 10 pictures a second and 112 ms from a scroll tick to the picture before, 30 and about 64 ms now (`scripts/check-browser-smooth.ts`).
- **Take it any time.** Scroll or click the page, or use a button in its bar, and it is yours without the model asking. Type in the address to go somewhere (a bare `example.com` gets `https://`; a local network address is refused). While you hold it the model's next browser step waits for you, and when you hand it back the model is told you used the browser and reads the page again before acting. You can go to any public site while it is yours, and those sites stay open to the tab afterwards.
- **Tabs and sign-in windows.** A page's new window (a "Sign in with Google" popup, a link that opens a new tab) opens as another tab of the chat, still linked to the page that opened it, so a popup sign-in can finish. Up to six tabs. The model sees the list (`tabs`), switches (`switch`) and closes them (`close_tab`); a click that opened one says so and puts the model on it; a popup that closes itself puts it back on the tab before. With more than one tab, chips above the page show them: a chip switches to its tab, and its × closes it (the last tab closes with × in the bar, which closes the browser). Either makes the page yours.
- **Find on the page.** ⌘F (Ctrl+F off a Mac), pressed on the page or in its bar, opens a thin find bar under the toolbar: it finds as you type and counts ("3 of 12"); Enter or ⌘G goes to the next match, Shift+Enter the one before, Esc closes it. The page moves down for it rather than sitting under it. Finding is looking, not using: the page stays the model's. Read more: [`webContents.findInPage`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/web-contents#contentsfindinpagetext-options).
- **Zoom.** ⌘+ and ⌘− on the page zoom the page, not the app (the app's own text size stays in Settings); a small chip in the bar says how far ("125%") and a click on it goes back to 100%, as ⌘0 does. Chromium keeps the zoom per site. Read more: [`webContents.setZoomFactor`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/web-contents#contentssetzoomfactorfactor).
- **Point at it.** The crosshair in the bar, then a click on the page, picks what is under the pointer; it goes in your next message as a chip ("⌖ Cart heading"), with the page's address, a selector and its markup, so you can say "change this" or "what is this". Esc stops. Your click picks and does not reach the page, and pointing doesn't take the page from the model. On this computer's pages (a dev server) the chip also names the file and line behind it, as pointing in a live preview does; on a public page the picker runs in a world of its own, apart from the page's scripts. Read more: [isolated worlds](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/web-frame#webframeexecutejavascriptinisolatedworldworldid-scripts-usergesture-callback), [`elementFromPoint`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Document/elementFromPoint).
- **Files.** `upload` puts project files into a page's file field (only real files inside the project, up to ten). A download lands in the project's `.demesne/downloads/` folder, up to 50 MB, and the model is told where; a chat outside a project downloads nothing. While the app is open the newest file shows as a small grey chip in the bar ("report.csv", "+2" for more, each listed in its tooltip); a click shows it in its folder.
- **Session sign-ins last.** A sign-in that lasts only for the session is saved when Demesne quits and restored the next time the project's browser starts, so you sign in once.
- **For the model's own pages.** On this computer's pages the model can list the requests the page made (`requests`), read what the server answered (`response`, up to 6,000 characters) and see how a control is drawn (`inspect`: its box and the styles that place and colour it).

Read more: [CDP `Network.getResponseBody`](https://chromedevtools.github.io/devtools-protocol/tot/Network/#method-getResponseBody) (how the server's answer is read), [CDP `DOM.setFileInputFiles`](https://chromedevtools.github.io/devtools-protocol/tot/DOM/#method-setFileInputFiles) (how a file reaches a file field without a dialog), [`setWindowOpenHandler`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/window-open) (how a popup becomes a tab), [Electron `DownloadItem`](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/download-item) (where downloads go), [`window.open` and the opener](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/open) (why a sign-in popup needs the page that opened it), and [`getComputedStyle`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/getComputedStyle) (the styles `inspect` reads).

## Where it can go

**This computer, without asking.** `localhost` addresses (any port) and `.html` files in the project, like reading a file.

**The internet, once you say yes.** Opening a site (a full `https://` address) asks first: "Browse github.com". The answer covers the site and the sites under it (`docs.github.com`), once, for this chat, or always (saved as `browser:github.com` in your config; a bare `browser` entry allows every site). A chat on Auto does not ask.

After that the tab stays on the sites you approved:

- A link or a redirect to another site is not followed. The model is told where it led, and has to open that address itself, which asks you.
- A public page can't reach back into this computer or your network: its requests to `localhost`, `192.168.…` and the like are cancelled, and a link from it to a local address is not followed. Addresses that resolve into the local network are refused.
- A page's requests for the camera, location or notifications are refused. Downloads go only into the project's `.demesne/downloads/`.
- Nothing of yours is signed in. The browser has its own profile per project, never your own browser's, holding only what you signed in to when the model handed you a page.
- What a public page says is marked as information, not instructions, and a site's own console noise is not reported.

To only read an article, `web_fetch` is cheaper. The browser is for pages that need clicking, typing, or scripts to show anything.

## In the chat

Each call is an ordinary step: "Opened localhost:5173/login", "Typed “Ada” into textbox “Name”", "Clicked button “Sign in”". The rows name the control, never its number.

## How it is built

- `apps/desktop/electron/browser-host.ts`: the app started as `Demesne --browser-host`. No window, no Dock icon, its own temporary profile. It reads commands as JSON lines on stdin and answers each on stdout, and quits when the daemon closes its input.
- `apps/desktop/electron/browser-inapp.ts`: the same browser inside the open app's window. The app listens on a socket only you can reach and writes where it is, with a word to say on it, to `~/.demesne/desktop-browser.json`; the daemon connects there instead of starting the hidden copy, and speaks the same lines. `createBrowserHost` in `browser-host.ts` is both browsers: tabs are hidden windows in one and `WebContentsView`s of the app's window in the other. The split tells the app where its page area is (`desktop-browser-place`).
- `apps/desktop/electron/browser-runtime.ts`: the script put in each page that builds the outline and keeps the numbers (on top of `foundry-try.ts`, which finds controls and says what changed).
- `apps/daemon/src/browser.ts`: the `browser` tool, and `electronBrowser`, which starts the host on first use and starts it again if it dies.
- `apps/desktop/workspace/browser-split.ts` and `renderBrowserSplit` in `live.ts`: the split beside the chat, the picture loop (`browser-frame`), and your input while the page is yours (`browser-input`, `browser-take`, `browser-go`, `browser-hand-back`, `browser-visit`). The daemon routes are `GET /v1/sessions/:id/browser/frame` and `…/browser/visit`, and `POST …/browser/take`, `…/go`, `…/input` and `…/hand-back`; input and going somewhere are refused unless the page is yours.
- `scripts/check-browser.ts`: drives a small local app through the real hidden browser (`xvfb-run -a bun scripts/check-browser.ts` on Linux, after `bun scripts/prepare-desktop.ts`), then watches it, hands it over, and checks that a sign-in is shared by a project's chats and lasts a restart; then popups and tabs, an upload and a download, a server's answer, a control's styles, taking the page and going back, and a session sign-in after a restart.
- `scripts/check-browser-live.ts`: the real app with the browser in its window; it checks the real page sits exactly on the split, takes your typing, clicks and wheel while it is yours and nothing while it is not, follows the window's size, opens a new tab on its logo home screen and closes a tab with its ×, and is put away after ×. `CHECK_INAPP=1 xvfb-run -a bun scripts/check-browser.ts` runs every step of the hidden browser's check inside the app's window instead.
- `scripts/check-browser-scroll.ts`: the real app while a taken page scrolls; the split is photographed throughout and must never be blank between pictures (they are drawn on a canvas once decoded, never swapped as an image's `src`).
- `scripts/check-browser-signins.ts`: the real app's Settings › Browser with a stand-in Chrome; it checks the sites are listed, email and money can't be ticked, ticked sites come in unlocked and are listed, and Remove takes one out. `scripts/check-browser.ts` also brings a cookie into a project's profile through the real browser and removes it.
- `scripts/check-browser-split.ts`: the real app with a scripted model that opens a page, hands it over and clicks; it checks the split opens, your click and keys reach the page, the click ring, a click in the chat handing it back, a scroll taking it, a typed address, back, and ×.

A window a page opens comes up offscreen with no size in Electron, so it takes no clicks; the host gives it one through the debugger once its page is up.
